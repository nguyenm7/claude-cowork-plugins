# Web & Desk Research Guide

Conduct research to find industry trends, data, and credible sources before drafting content.

---

## When to Research

**Always research before writing**:
- Thought leadership pieces (need external validation)
- SEO content (need to understand what's ranking)
- Trend pieces (need current data)
- Any content making industry claims

**Research depth by content type**:
| Content Type | Research Depth |
|--------------|----------------|
| Thought leadership | Deep — 30-60 min |
| Blog (SEO) | Medium — 15-30 min |
| Case study | Light — validate claims |
| LinkedIn post | Light — find one hook stat |
| Social | Minimal — unless data-driven |

---

## Research Workflow

### Step 1: Frame the Research Questions

Before searching, write down 3-5 questions you need answered:

**Example for "Build vs Buy" post**:
1. What do industry analysts say about build vs buy for internal tools?
2. What's the average cost of maintaining internal tools?
3. How much engineering time do companies waste on internal tooling?
4. What are the failure rates for internal tool projects?
5. Are there recent surveys on this topic?

### Step 2: Search Credible Sources First

**Search in this order** (highest credibility first):

1. **Industry analyst reports**
   - Search: `[topic] site:gartner.com OR site:forrester.com OR site:mckinsey.com`
   - Search: `[topic] "Gartner" OR "Forrester" filetype:pdf`

2. **Academic / research institutions**
   - Search: `[topic] site:hbr.org`
   - Search: `[topic] site:mit.edu OR site:stanford.edu`

3. **Major publications**
   - Search: `[topic] site:wsj.com OR site:nytimes.com OR site:economist.com`
   - Search: `[topic] site:techcrunch.com OR site:theinformation.com`

4. **Industry surveys & benchmarks**
   - Search: `[topic] survey 2025 OR 2024`
   - Search: `[topic] benchmark report`
   - Search: `[topic] "state of" report`

5. **Recognized thought leaders**
   - Search: `[topic] site:reforge.com OR site:lennysnewsletter.com`
   - Search: `[topic] [known expert name]`

### Step 3: Validate What You Find

For every stat or claim, ask:
- [ ] **Who produced this?** (Credible org or random blog?)
- [ ] **When?** (Is it current? <3 years for fast-moving topics)
- [ ] **How?** (Is there methodology? Sample size?)
- [ ] **Primary source?** (Or are they citing someone else?)

If a stat appears on multiple blogs but none link to original research → **Don't use it**.

### Step 4: Document Your Findings

Create a research notes section with:

```markdown
## Research Notes: [Topic]

### Key Stats Found
- [Stat] — Source: [Name], [Year], [URL]
- [Stat] — Source: [Name], [Year], [URL]

### Trends / Themes
- [Observation from multiple sources]
- [Observation]

### Expert Quotes
- "[Quote]" — [Name], [Title], [Source]

### Gaps (couldn't find)
- [Question that remains unanswered]

### Sources to Cite
1. [Full citation with URL]
2. [Full citation with URL]
```

## Credible Source Quick Reference

### Tier 1: Always Cite
| Category | Sources |
|----------|---------|
| Analysts | Gartner, Forrester, McKinsey, Bain, IDC |
| Academic | HBR, MIT Sloan, Stanford GSB |
| Research orgs | Pew, Qualtrics XM Institute |
| Major press | WSJ, NYT, Economist, FT |

### Tier 2: Cite with Context
| Category | Sources |
|----------|---------|
| Tech press | TechCrunch, The Information, Protocol |
| Industry reports | ChartMogul, OpenView, ProfitWell |
| Expert blogs | Reforge, Lenny's Newsletter, SVPG |
| Company research | (with methodology disclosed) |

### Tier 3: Use Carefully
| Category | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| Individual blogs | Only if recognized expert |
| Social posts | Only as illustration, not evidence |
| Competitor content | Verify independently |

## Red Flags in Research

🚩 **Stat with no source link** — Find the original or don't use

🚩 **"Studies show..." with no specific study** — Too vague to cite

🚩 **Impressive round numbers** (exactly 10x, 50%) — Often estimates, not data

🚩 **Source is selling related product** — May be biased

🚩 **Multiple blogs cite same stat, none have original** — Likely misquoted

🚩 **Data > 3 years old in fast-moving space** — Find updated version

## Integrating Research into Content

### For Thought Leadership
- Lead with a surprising stat or trend
- Use external data to validate your thesis
- Reference industry experts to build credibility
- End with forward-looking insight

**Example**:
> Gartner predicts that by 2026, 80% of enterprises will have deployed AI-powered customer feedback tools—up from just 15% in 2023. The build-vs-buy decision is no longer theoretical.

### For SEO Content
- Check what top-ranking content cites
- Include more/better sources than competitors
- Use stats in headings when possible
- Add "data updated [month year]" for freshness

### For Case Studies
- Use industry benchmarks to contextualize customer results
- "While the average company sees X, [Customer] achieved Y"
- Reference market trends that make the customer's challenge relatable

## Research Checklist

Before moving to drafting:

- [ ] Answered key research questions
- [ ] Found 2-3 credible stats to anchor the piece
- [ ] Verified stats trace to primary sources
- [ ] Documented sources with full citations
- [ ] Identified any gaps to fill with customer data (Wisdom MCP)
- [ ] Noted expert quotes worth including
