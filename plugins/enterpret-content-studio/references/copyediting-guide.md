# Copyediting & Prose Polish Guide

Fine-tooth-comb editing to ensure content sounds human, not like AI slop.

---

## The Anti-Slop Philosophy

AI-generated content often sounds "correct" but lifeless. It uses safe phrases, hedges constantly, and lacks the texture of human thought.

**Our goal**: Content that sounds like a smart person wrote it — with opinions, rhythm, and personality.

---

## Red Flags: AI Slop Indicators

### Hollow Intensifiers
These words add nothing but fake emphasis:

❌ **Avoid**:
- "very" / "really" / "truly" / "incredibly"
- "absolutely" / "definitely" / "certainly"
- "significantly" / "substantially"
- "robust" / "comprehensive" / "holistic"
- "cutting-edge" / "state-of-the-art" / "best-in-class"
- "leverage" / "utilize" (just say "use")
- "empower" / "enable" (usually meaningless)

✅ **Instead**: Be specific or delete the intensifier entirely.

**Before**: "This incredibly powerful solution significantly improves outcomes."
**After**: "This cuts analysis time by 80%."

### Weasel Phrases
Vague language that avoids commitment:

❌ **Avoid**:
- "In today's fast-paced world..."
- "It's no secret that..."
- "At the end of the day..."
- "When it comes to..."
- "In order to..." (just say "to")
- "The fact that..."
- "It goes without saying..."
- "Needless to say..."

✅ **Instead**: Just make the point directly.

### Throat-Clearing
Unnecessary wind-up before the actual content:

❌ **Before**: "In this blog post, we're going to explore the various ways in which customer feedback can be analyzed and understood by modern teams."

✅ **After**: "Customer feedback analysis has changed. Here's what works now."

### Passive Voice (When Hiding the Actor)
Passive voice hides who's doing what:

❌ **Before**: "Insights are delivered to stakeholders on a regular basis."
✅ **After**: "The team shares insights with stakeholders weekly."

(Passive voice is fine when the actor is unknown or unimportant.)

### List Dependency
Over-reliance on bullet points instead of prose:

❌ **Before**:
"Benefits include:
- Faster insights
- Better accuracy
- Reduced manual work"

✅ **After**: "Teams get insights faster, with better accuracy and far less manual work."

### Empty Transitions
Transitions that don't actually connect ideas:

❌ **Avoid**:
- "That being said..."
- "With that in mind..."
- "Moving on..."
- "Furthermore..."
- "Additionally..."

✅ **Instead**: Either the ideas connect naturally, or use a specific transition that shows the relationship.

## Sentence-Level Editing

### Cut the Fat
Read every sentence and ask: "What can I delete without losing meaning?"

**Before**: "One of the things that makes Enterpret unique is the fact that it is able to learn your specific business language."

**After**: "Enterpret learns your business language."

### Vary Sentence Length
Monotonous rhythm = boring reading.

**Monotonous**: "Teams struggle with feedback. They use manual processes. This takes too much time. It creates inconsistent results."

**Better**: "Teams struggle with feedback. Manual processes eat hours every week, and the results are inconsistent at best. Something has to change."

### Front-Load the Point
Put the most important information first:

**Before**: "After months of manual work and countless hours spent tagging tickets, the team finally realized they needed a better solution."

**After**: "The team needed a better solution. Months of manual tagging had proven that."

### Use Concrete Language
Replace abstractions with specifics:

**Abstract**: "Significant improvements in operational efficiency"
**Concrete**: "Cut ticket processing time from 3 hours to 20 minutes"

**Abstract**: "Enhanced customer understanding"
**Concrete**: "Finally knew why customers were churning"

---

## Paragraph-Level Editing

### One Idea Per Paragraph
If a paragraph covers multiple ideas, split it.

### Strong First Sentences
The first sentence should make the reader want the second.

**Weak**: "There are many factors to consider when building a voice of customer program."

**Strong**: "Most VoC programs fail in the first year. Here's why yours won't."

### Smooth Transitions
Each paragraph should connect to the previous one. If you can't explain the connection, the structure needs work.

---

## Document-Level Editing

### Check the Arc
Read the full piece and ask:
- Is there a clear beginning, middle, end?
- Does the intro promise something the conclusion delivers?
- Is there a single, clear takeaway?

### Cut Repetition
Search for key phrases. If you've made a point twice, cut one.

### Read Aloud
Actually read it out loud. You'll catch:
- Awkward phrasing
- Sentences that are too long
- Rhythm problems
- Words that don't sound like you

---

## Voice Checklist

Before publishing, verify the content sounds like Enterpret:

- [ ] **Confident, not arrogant**: Makes claims, backs them up
- [ ] **Specific, not vague**: Numbers, names, examples
- [ ] **Direct, not hedged**: Says what it means
- [ ] **Human, not corporate**: Sounds like a person, not a press release
- [ ] **Opinionated, not bland**: Takes a stance
- [ ] **Varied rhythm**: Mix of sentence lengths
- [ ] **Active voice**: Clear actors doing things

## Common Fixes

| AI Slop | Human Version |
|---------|---------------|
| "utilize" | "use" |
| "in order to" | "to" |
| "at this point in time" | "now" |
| "due to the fact that" | "because" |
| "has the ability to" | "can" |
| "in the event that" | "if" |
| "a large number of" | "many" |
| "the vast majority of" | "most" |
| "on a daily basis" | "daily" |
| "conduct an investigation" | "investigate" |
| "make a decision" | "decide" |
| "provides support for" | "supports" |

## The Final Pass

### Speed Read
Skim the piece quickly. Note where your eye catches or your brain stumbles. Those spots need work.

### Cut 10%
Challenge yourself to cut 10% of the word count without losing meaning. Almost always possible.

### Headlines & Subheads Test
Read only the headlines and subheads. Do they tell a coherent story?

### First & Last Sentence Test
Read the first sentence and last sentence. Do they feel connected?

## Examples: Before & After

### Example 1: Fluffy Opening

**Before**:
"In today's increasingly competitive business landscape, it has become more important than ever for companies to truly understand and listen to the voice of their customers in order to stay ahead of the curve and drive meaningful business outcomes."

**After**:
"The companies that listen to customers win. The ones that don't, lose. It's that simple."

### Example 2: Feature Description

**Before**:
"Enterpret's cutting-edge AI technology leverages advanced machine learning capabilities to provide comprehensive and holistic analysis of customer feedback data, enabling teams to unlock powerful insights that drive actionable outcomes."

**After**:
"Enterpret's AI reads your customer feedback and tells you what matters. No tagging. No spreadsheets. Just answers."

### Example 3: Results Section

**Before**:
"The implementation of Enterpret resulted in significant improvements across a variety of key performance indicators, including but not limited to time savings, accuracy improvements, and enhanced team productivity."

**After**:
"After implementing Enterpret: analysis time dropped 83%. Accuracy went up. And the team got their Fridays back."

## Editor Mindset

When editing, channel these personas:

**The Skeptic**: "Prove it. Show me the evidence."

**The Impatient Reader**: "Get to the point. I have 30 seconds."

**The Confused Customer**: "I don't understand this jargon. Explain it simply."

**The Competitor**: "Is this actually different, or just marketing speak?"

If the content survives all four, it's ready.
